The concept of my collection, “The Experience” is about the freshness of experience. Some things are much different from the first time you interact with it, and that same feeling may never come back, be lost. My subject is my experience and journey with an arcade rhythm game, Maimai, that I have been playing. In the collection, the more you scroll, the blurrier the past records become. The reason I chose this as my subject is that it is a prime example, and one that I have most connection with in the field of never feeling the same. The longer I play the game, the more experienced I am. The more experienced I am, the more different the gameplay experience becomes. If I play the same song I played 3 years ago when I started, I would feel completely different. The sense of achievement, the freshness and the excitement is lost. This specific collection may not resonate with most people, but most people have the same issue, losing the same feeling when you started. 

In my collection, I have collected 18 pictures, an audio clip that I recorded during my journey and a record of some messages between my friends related to my experience. These records mostly recorded my growth in the game, how I was getting better and better. But while I was getting better, some things were also lost. What those records couldn’t hold was how I felt that moment, something that I would not encounter again even If I do the same now. The difference is the experience that I accumulated. Since I got better at the game, I started to aim at getting better. When I play to get better, I start to lose the fun that I had when I first played the game, as there are more frustration and stress the more I play to improve. The accumulated experience changes the gameplay experience, and the push to improve also takes part in that change. This is something that even the records could not hold and is lost in translation. That is why my collection is called “The Experience”. It summarises what the records represent and opens up a conversation on the relationship of experience and freshness by taking a deep dive into the lost feeling in these records.

Although things are not the same, it doesn’t mean that the game is no longer fun. I will still play Maimai in the foreseeable future. However, it will be fun in a different way than what I started with. Even if it becomes tasteless, I can always find something new to start again, but it would not be the same. The concept of loss of freshness can also apply to the larger picture. Despite how many new things are presented to us in this age, since we have already lived quite some time, we become numb to this sense of freshness. This loss is something we don’t normally notice in our daily life.
